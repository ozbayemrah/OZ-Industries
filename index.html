01The orbital bottleneck

Satellites are lost to small failures. Fixing them from Earth is hard.

Expensive spacecraft go out of service, or become debris, because one mechanism did not move. The tools to reach and repair them exist only in parts, and the people who would operate them work through a delayed link.

Failure mode 01

Stuck mechanisms

Jammed deployment hinges, solar arrays that never unfold, antennas that never extend. The satellite works, but it can't do its job.

Typical causeMinor mechanical fault
Failure mode 02

The teleoperation gap

Ground operators see where the robot was, not where it is. Signal delay plus a dense, unfamiliar interface overloads people at the moment precision matters most.

RiskCollision during rendezvous
Failure mode 03

Rigid hardware

Conventional robotic arms are heavy and single-purpose. They struggle to grip smooth, irregular or non-cooperative surfaces through vacuum and thermal cycling.

ConstraintMass · volume · grip

02.1Capture & holdREMORA × gecko grip

How we grab a satellite that was never built to be grabbed.

Most satellites in orbit have no docking port and no grapple fixture. Their hulls are smooth composite, glass or painted panels, so magnets and hooks have nothing to hold. REMORA's capture pads borrow the gecko's trick: microscopic wedge structures that stick through van der Waals forces once they are pulled sideways, and let go when that pull is removed.

Step 01

Match, then approach

MONOLITH syncs REMORA to the client's tumble, so the claw arrives with almost no relative motion.

Step 02

Soft touch

Pads covered in microscopic wedges meet the hull. They need almost no pressing force, so a free-floating client isn't pushed away.

Step 03

Shear lock

The arms draw the pads toward each other. Loaded sideways, the wedges bend flat, the contact area jumps, and the grip locks.

Step 04

Hold, then peel

Holding takes no power. To let go, the arms relax the sideways pull and peel the pads off, leaving nothing behind on the client.

Holding powerPassive · 0 W
Client surfaceSmooth, non-magnetic, no fixture
EnvironmentVacuum · thermal cycling
StatusConcept research · C2

Design goals, not test results. Bench tests of the pad concept are part of the planned R&D work.
